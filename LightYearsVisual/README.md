# LightYearsVisual

Standalone static library (`LightYearsVisual`, namespace `ly::visual`) for procedural, sprite-free visuals drawn with SFML shapes, vertex arrays and GLSL `#version 120` shaders. It depends only on SFML and nlohmann_json, not on `LightYearsEngine` or the game. Status: V1 foundation, first game integration pass, and V3 procedural player ships (see "Ships (V3)", "Game integration" and `docs/VISUAL_LIBRARY_PLAN.md`).

## Layers

`VisualPipeline` owns background, world, sprite and energy targets plus a nebula target when `nebula.resolutionDivisor` is above 1. It composites background (gradient, nebula, stars), world, sprites, energy, bloom, flash and vignette in that order. All drawing coordinates are output pixels; the pipeline maps them onto each layer.

| Layer | Resolution | Look | Draw with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Background | full | sky: gradient, host star, planet, Milky Way band, dust, star field with parallax, pulsar; receives the composited nebula; lensed at Sgr A* | `DrawGradient`, `DrawHostStar`, `DrawPlanet`, `DrawDustCloud`, `StarField`, `DrawPulsar`, `SetLens` |
| Nebula | `ceil(output / nebula.resolutionDivisor)` | Milky Way band and emission nebula, smoothly upscaled and premultiplied over Background | `DrawMilkyWay` and `DrawEmissionNebula` on `Target(Layer::Nebula)`, then `CompositeNebula()` |
| World | virtual (`virtualHeight`, e.g. 240 rows), upscaled nearest | crisp pixels, hard silhouettes, outlines, optional palette dither | `ShapeBrush` (use `hardEdges`), `DrawPlanet` |
| Sprites | same virtual resolution as World, upscaled nearest, drawn over World | pixel-scaled content that keeps its own colors, never dithered (game sprite actors) | any `RenderTarget` draw |
| Energy | full, additive | tiny bright core, limited halo, dark surroundings, bloom source | `SubmitGlow`, `SubmitTrail`, `SubmitFlash` |

Why two world-resolution layers: the palette dither snaps every pixel to the active color script, which is right for procedural art (planet, shapes) but recolors authored sprites. Procedural content goes to `Layer::World` (dithered), sprites to `Layer::Sprites` (original colors, composited premultiplied so soft edges are not darkened twice).

Camera jitter: world-layer content shows sub-pixel camera steps as shimmer. `pipeline.SnapWorldView(gameView)` returns the camera view with its top-left corner snapped to the virtual pixel grid and widened to the whole texture; set it on the World/Sprites targets and derive ship and glow positions from it so they stay aligned. Full-resolution backdrop parallax uses the continuous game view, without rounding the planet center to the gameplay pixel grid.

Frame: `pipeline.BeginFrame()`, draw the gradient, host star and planet into Background, draw the Milky Way band and emission nebula into `Target(Layer::Nebula)`, call `CompositeNebula()`, then draw dust, stars and pulsar into Background and set the lens. Draw World/Sprites and submit energy before `pipeline.Compose(target)`. Call `Resize(size)` when the output size changes.

## Backdrop

The sky is sampled from the galaxy model (`galaxy::ComputeSky`, see `GalaxyModel.h`) at the place on the campaign route. The game's `ProceduralBackdrop` builds the place from a route stop (`SetRouteStop`, seeded from the stop id) or from a bare position (`SetGalacticPosition`); the gallery does the same from its progress value. Stars use blackbody colour, and their brightness follows the magnitude system. There is no twinkle; variable stars change brightness instead. The star field and band are rebuilt when the position or the `starField` and `milkyWay` settings change. Draw order, far to near:

1. gradient (darkened toward black);
2. host star (`DrawHostStar`): temperature and luminosity from the route stop, distance from the first planet's semi-major axis, else the temperate distance sqrt(L) AU;
3. backdrop planet, palette role by the first planet's equilibrium temperature (above 700 K `combatWarm`, 200 to 700 K `planet`, below 200 K `player`);
4. shattered moon debris around the planet (`ShatteredMoon`), when the stop has a planet and the seeded chance hits;
5. Milky Way band and emission nebula (`DrawEmissionNebula`, Balmer and [N II]/[S II] lines, [O III] only around hot ionizers) into the nebula target, present when arm proximity is above 0.3 and composited premultiplied;
6. dust cloud (`DrawDustCloud`): multiplicative extinction, then light reflected from the emission centre or a stray point; present on dense or arm sky with a seeded 70 % chance;
7. blackbody star field, moving with warp;
8. pulsar (`DrawPulsar`): procedural stops past 20 % of the route, with a seeded chance and a period of 0.7 to 3 s;
9. the Sgr A* lens (`SetLens`) over the whole background, only at the final stop: a point lens with a shadow and a photon ring.

The emission and dust clouds replace the old palette nebula clouds. The host star and the planet sit behind the band and the nebula on purpose (art direction); the stars are drawn on top.

Settings: `physicalNebula` (`NebulaSettings`: emission radius and density, dust depth and size, reflection, parallax), `anomalies` (pulsar and moon chances, lens radii and ring brightness) and `warp` (`maxBeta`, `rampSeconds`, `fieldOfViewRad`). `ProceduralBackdrop::SetWarp(beta, direction)`, forwarded by `GameVisualPipeline::SetWarp`, eases the ship speed toward beta and feeds `StarField::SetMotion`; the dev option `--warp` uses it, gameplay does not call it yet.

Route data: `LightYearsGame/assets/content/data/galaxy_route.json` holds the real exoplanet hosts from the NASA Exoplanet Archive (see `docs/GALAXY_ROUTE_DATA.md`). Acknowledgement: "This research has made use of the NASA Exoplanet Archive, which is operated by the California Institute of Technology, under contract with the National Aeronautics and Space Administration under the Exoplanet Exploration Program." The game appends 14 procedural stops with `GalaxyRoute::AppendProceduralStops(14, 7)`, ending at Sgr A*. `--galaxy-stop <index>` selects a stop (0 is Sol).

The gallery has no route: it uses `galaxy::RoutePosition(progress)` from the Sun (progress 0) straight in to Sgr A* (progress 1), and treats progress 0.999 and above as Sgr A*. Its title and capture line list the active features (`emission`, `dust`, `pulsar`, `moon`, `lens`, or `none`).

The game's backdrop uses a deterministic composition from each stop id: the planet may enter from either side, with small position/scale changes, and the band angle/offset varies around the configured values. The resolved band stars share those same band settings; planet lighting, cloud lighting and comet tails share the host-star position. The layout stays fixed while fighting and is recomputed from the same seed on settings reload. Shipped settings keep the warm/cool Milky Way colours, use broad dust and star-cloud shapes with sparse fine stars, and limit background brightness to 0.46. Planet and sparse nearby stars have stronger camera parallax than the distant band; background motion does not animate the whole galaxy or feed back into gameplay.

For normal flight, nearby stars and a sparse layer of dim dust motes provide the faster depth reference; the planet follows more slowly and the galactic band is the distant anchor. The game's `nearField` uses only the mote path of `NearField` (no rock ramp), at small output-pixel sizes and below the background brightness cap. The motes gently drift at rest and follow continuous camera motion with stronger parallax while moving. `nearField.enabled` can disable this layer during hot reload; the gallery retains its rock-and-mote demonstration.

## Color script

A script (`assets/palettes/*.json`) holds named ramps (ordered stops, shadow to light) and named roles pointing at ramps (`background`, `ambient`, `ambient2`, `combatWarm`, `player`, `enemy`, `hull`, `planet`; roles are free-form). Shadows are authored hue-shifted (turquoise to deep blue, pink to maroon), never gray. A missing ramp or role samples as loud magenta. Ramp stops are `"#RRGGBB"` strings (evenly spaced) or `{"t": 0.3, "color": "#RRGGBB"}`. Switch at runtime with `assets.Scripts().SetActive(name)` or `CycleActive()`.

## Luminance tiers and effect budget

`LuminanceTier`: Background < Environment < Decorative < EnemyProjectile < Player. `PipelineSettings::tierMaxBrightness` caps the peak channel of every energy draw of a tier; the caps must not decrease with tier (a settings file that violates this is rejected). Over-bright draws are scaled down, not dropped. Energy draws are queued, then at `Compose` sorted by survival rank (Player, EnemyProjectile, Environment, Background, Decorative) and drawn until `maxEnergyDraws` or `maxEnergyAreaRatio` (sum of draw area / output area) is reached; the rest is culled, so decorative effects go first. `pipeline.Stats()` reports submitted, drawn, culled and clamped counts. Bloom is computed from the energy layer only, so only intentional light blooms. Bloom, flash and vignette strength are settings.

## Settings and hot reload

`assets/pipeline.json` maps onto `PipelineSettings` (`virtualHeight`, `nebula.resolutionDivisor`, dither, bloom threshold, strength and levels, vignette, flash scale, energy budget, tier caps, and the `planet` section: `enabled`, `screenX`/`screenY` (fractions of the output at the camera start), `radius` (fraction of output height), `parallax`, `brightness`, `saturation`, `opacity` (0–1, missing-key default 1), `rotationSpeed`, `dither`), plus the `physicalNebula`, `anomalies` and `warp` sections described under Backdrop. The `PipelineSettings` code default and missing-key fallback for older files are 1; values clamp to 1–8. The shipped `pipeline.json` selects 4 after verification, rendering each nebula axis at one-quarter output resolution. `VisualAssets` owns the loaded settings, shaders (`assets/shaders/<name>.frag`) and palettes. `assets.Poll(dt)` checks file timestamps twice a second and `assets.Reload(true)` forces a reload (F8 in the gallery). A file that fails to parse or compile keeps its previous working version and the error text lands in `ReloadReport::errors`. Settings are plain data: edit `assets.Settings()` at runtime; a reload of `pipeline.json` overwrites such edits.

## Gallery

```
LightYearsVisualGallery [--assets <dir>] [--script <name>] [--galaxy <progress>] [--backdrop-only]
LightYearsVisualGallery --capture <out.png> [--script <name>] [--frames N] [--size WxH] [--stress] [--galaxy <progress>] [--backdrop-only] [--warp <beta>]
```

The scene shows all six ship definitions at full size (row 1: shields up, one in afterburner, a hit flash and shield ripple cycling through), the first ship at four damage stages plus two ships with mixed port states (row 2: unequipped, cooling down, ready, active, activation burst), and a game-size strip (row 3, about 20 to 28 world pixels across). The window title shows the active nebula scale (`1/1` is full resolution). Keys: `Tab` color script, `B` bloom, `D` dither, `V` virtual resolution, `N` nebula divisor (cycles 1/2/4/8), `Space` energy-budget stress, `Up`/`Down` bloom strength, `F` backdrop only (sky alone, without planet, ships and energy), `W` warp toggle (0 or 0.9 upward), `PageUp`/`PageDown` galaxy progress by 0.05, `Home`/`End` by 0.005, `F8` reload, `Esc` quit. `--galaxy <0..1>` sets the starting place on the route (0 = Sun, 1 = Sgr A*); `--backdrop-only` starts with the sky alone and `--warp <beta>` starts at that ship speed. The title shows the progress, the distance to the centre, the nearest arm, the active features and the warp target, and capture mode prints the same line after the save message. The gallery reads the source `assets/` folder directly (`--assets` overrides) so edits hot-reload without rebuilding. Capture mode needs no window: it renders N fixed 1/60 s frames and saves the composited image.

Build: part of the root CMake project; the gallery is behind `LIGHTYEARS_BUILD_VISUAL_GALLERY` (default ON).

## Ships (V3)

A ship is data (`assets/ships/*.json`, one ship per file, hot-reloaded with the other assets, `assets.Ships()`), drawn by `ShipRenderer::Draw(pipeline, definition, state)`. The library never sees game types: a definition has an opaque `id` and optional opaque `bindings` (the game lists its ship content ids there; `Ships().FindByBinding(id)`), so the id-to-visual mapping lives in these files and not in game content.

Definition fields (local units, nose toward -y, about 100 units across): `scale` (multiplier on the caller's scale), `roles` (palette roles sampled by part roles: `hull`, `plate`, `accent`, `glass`), `shield` (bubble semi-axes), `smoke`, `parts`, `lines`, `lights`, `engines`, `ports` (exactly 4: ability slots Q, E, F, R). A part is a polygon with `name`, `points`, `role` (`hull`, `plate`, `dark`, `accent`, `trim`, `glass`), `shade` (ramp stop offset), `gap` (dark outline over its neighbors), `crack`, `seed`, `detach` (damage stage from which it is missing; a pair is right/left) and `mirror` (points describe the right half; the left copy is expanded at load with a `_L` name suffix; the lit side keeps its shade, the other side is one stop darker). Colors are ramp stops, so every hull color is on the dither palette; the dither shader leaves exact palette colors flat. `size` (bounds of the parts) is computed at load so a caller can fit the design to another footprint.

`ShipVisualState` is filled by the caller each frame: position (output pixels), rotation, scale (output pixels per local unit), `hullFraction`, `shieldFraction`, `hitFlash` and `shieldHit` (1 to 0 timers), `thrust`, `afterburner`, `ghost` (spawn protection flicker), four `ShipPortState` (`equipped`, `category`, `cooldownFraction` 0 just started to 1 done, `isReady`, `isActive`, `justActivated` 1 to 0), `time` and `seed`. Nothing is retained between frames.

Behavior: hull and outline go to the dithered World layer; engines (`SubmitTrail` plus `SubmitGlow`), lights, ports and sparks are Player-tier energy (sparks Decorative); the shield is a custom additive draw (`SubmitEnergy`, shader `shield.frag`). Damage stage from the hull fraction: above 75% clean; above 50% plates one stop darker and cracks; above 25% sparks, a dark scar where a plate is missing, more cracks; below that more missing parts, flickering lights and ports, dark smoke wisps drawn under the hull. All of it is deterministic from part seeds, the ship seed and `time`. Shield: faint shell and rim following the fraction (flickers when nearly empty, nothing at zero), rim brightens with a ripple on `shieldHit`. Ports: unequipped is a dark socket; equipped is lit in the category color (palette roles `portPrimary`, `portOffense`, `portDefense`, `portMovement`, `portUtility`, defined in each palette); cooling down is a dim socket with a pie filling clockwise and a dim glow growing with progress; ready pulses; active is bright; `justActivated` adds a bright burst and an expanding ring. Level of detail: ships under about 34 world pixels across drop the dark gaps, under 28 the panel lines.

## Game integration (pass 1)

The library stays independent of the engine. `LightYearsEngine` only knows the small `WorldRenderPipeline` interface (`framework/WorldRenderPipeline.h`); `LightYearsGame` implements it (`presentation/visual/GameVisualPipeline`) on top of this library. `World::Render` draws `RenderLayer::Background` into the Background layer (the pipeline paints it itself, so old PNG background actors are skipped, not deleted) and every other render layer into the undithered pixel Sprites layer with the pixel-snapped camera view, composes onto the window, then draws the HUD at full resolution. `ProceduralBackdrop` paints the sky (gradient, Milky Way band, nebula and stars, see "Backdrop") into Background and draws the small distant planet last, over them. The planet is tuned by the `planet` section of `pipeline.json`, anchored to the camera start of each world, and follows continuous camera parallax with an unsnapped center. Its shader supplies tone bands and planet-local Bayer dither sampled at the world-pixel scale for the surface style. The center and the soft one-output-pixel limb stay continuous; shipped opacity 0.35 blends it into the background haze. `EnergyLightPresenter` submits engine and projectile glows from a read-only look at the world.

Keys in the game: `F7` cycles the color script, `F8` toggles the pipeline (starts ON; a failed asset load keeps it OFF with a log line), `F9` forces an asset reload; files are also polled every frame. The game currently reads `assets/` from the source tree (`LY_VISUAL_ASSETS_DIR`); a packaged copy step can replace that later. Dev command line: `LightYearsGame.exe --visual-capture <png> [--capture-frames N] [--visual-off] [--visual-script <name>] [--visual-player-hull <0..1>] [--visual-ship <0..5>] [--galaxy-stop <index>] [--warp <beta>]` (`--galaxy-stop` picks a route stop, 0 is Sol; `--warp` starts an upward warp, clamped to `warp.maxBeta`) (`--visual-player-hull` only changes the hull fraction the procedural ship shows; `--visual-ship` starts with that player ship).

Player ship (V3): `ShipVisualPresenter` reads the current player ship (transform, velocity, health and shield fractions, afterburner, invulnerability, the four ability slots through the same `GetAbility(slot)` read the HUD bar uses) and draws it with `ShipRenderer`. Hits are noticed by watching the health and shield fractions drop, so nothing is bound or written back. The selected ship content id picks the definition through the `bindings` of the ship JSON. While the pipeline is ON the world skips that ship's sprite through `WorldRenderPipeline::HidesActor` (a draw-time decision of the pipeline; the actor's render flags, physics and collision are untouched, and with the pipeline OFF the old path runs unchanged). The visual is fitted to the sprite footprint by area times the definition `scale` (1.25 in the shipped data, an art choice for readability; set it to 1 for the sprite footprint). The old player engine glow in `EnergyLightPresenter` is gone; the ship renderer owns it.
